# Cross-domain validation — results

Companion to the [pre-registration](preregistration.md). The predictions were fixed
before this measurement; the outcomes below are reported as measured, without
retroactive adjustment. Raw data: `output/crossdomain.json`.
Reproduce with `node src/build_crossdomain.ts`.

## Outcome of the pre-registered hypotheses

Evaluated at the largest measured size (n = 2·10⁴), as specified in the driver.

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Measured | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1** — forward scan ⟹ δ > 0 | δ > +40, D < 1 | `linear_reduce`: δ = **+90.0‰**, D = 0.024 | Supported |
| **H2** — root return ⟹ δ < 0 | δ < 0 | `tree_reduce`: δ = **−67.5‰**, D = 0.048 | Supported |
| **H3** — quadratic ⟹ D > 5 (any family) | D > 5 | `self_join`: D = **70 → 958** (n = 10³ → 2·10⁴) | Supported |
| **H4** — random probing ⟹ δ ≈ 0 | \|δ\| < 10 | `hash_probe`: δ ∈ [−5.6, +1.2]‰ across n | Supported |

All four hypotheses are supported, each with a wide margin relative to its decision
boundary: +90‰ against a +40 bound; −67.5‰ against 0; a load two to three orders of
magnitude above D = 5; and |δ| ≤ 5.6 against a band of 10.

## Stability across problem sizes

Over 20 series (4 workloads × 5 sizes, n from 10³ to 2·10⁴), using the **thresholds
calibrated on the sorting atlas** (D ≈ 5; δ = 0) and the **same reference**
E(ref) = Merge Sort on random input:

- **Load regime consistent in 20/20 series**: each workload stays on the same side of the
  D ≈ 5 boundary at every n (`linear_reduce`, `tree_reduce`, `hash_probe`: normal;
  `self_join`: inflated).
- **Sign of δ consistent in 20/20 series**: forward scan +, root return −, random
  access ≈ 0, `self_join` +.

## Families, not algorithms

The thresholds calibrated on sorting transfer without recalibration to workloads outside
sorting. Re-organizing the corpus by access-pattern family:

| Family | Sorting instances (n = 10⁴) | Non-sorting instance | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward scan | Merge (+73), QuickSort (+73) | `linear_reduce` (+90) | δ > 0 |
| Root return | Heapsort (−21) | `tree_reduce` (−67) | δ < 0 |
| Near-zero trend | Radix (+3, multi-pass) | `hash_probe` (≈ 0, random) | δ ≈ 0 — see *Limitations* |
| Quadratic overload | Insertion (D = 129), naive QuickSort (D = 112) | `self_join` (D = 70–958) | D ≫ 5 |

## Observations

1. **The non-sorting workloads are more prototypical instances of their families than
   the sorting algorithms.** `linear_reduce` reaches δ = +90‰ — the maximal monotone ramp
   over 10 bands — above Merge Sort (+73); `tree_reduce` reaches −67‰, well below Heapsort
   (−21). Linear reduction and bottom-up folding are monotone sweeps without the recursive
   structure that partially folds the access pointer in the sorting algorithms.
2. **`self_join` is structurally O(n²).** Unlike naive QuickSort, which requires sorted
   or reversed input to degenerate, the nested loop is quadratic for any content. The
   same D > 5 boundary flags both cases: it detects an explosion in the number of
   accesses regardless of whether it is accidental (input-triggered degeneration) or
   inherent to the algorithm.
3. **The quadratic-overload family is heterogeneous in δ** (`self_join` +45,
   Insertion +48, naive QuickSort on reversed input ≈ 0, on nearly sorted input −36). The
   family is defined by D and spread along δ: D indicates *whether* the workload is
   inflated, and δ carries information about the shape of the trajectory.
4. **D and δ behave as independent axes.** `self_join` has D = 958 and δ = +45
   simultaneously: an extreme increase in load does not shift the drift. This argues
   against the two features being re-encodings of the same quantity.

## Methodological checks

Before reporting, the experiment was subjected to five checks aimed at invalidating it.
Checks 4 and 5 were one-off measurements and are not regenerated by the scripts in this
repository; the systematic robustness tests are in [`robustness/`](../robustness/README.md).

1. **Thresholds chosen after seeing the data?** D ≈ 5 and the |δ| < 10 band come from the
   sorting atlas and were not recalibrated for this test. The H1 bound (δ > +40) was
   fixed a priori and is stricter than the family definition requires; the relevant
   discriminator is the sign (δ > 0). The value +40 is arbitrary and should not be read
   as a principal threshold.
2. **Is δ ≈ 0 specific to random access?** No — see *Limitations* below.
3. **Do some workloads favour δ by construction?** Yes. `linear_reduce` and
   `tree_reduce` are canonical, engineered instances of their families: they confirm the
   *sign* by design and are not adversarial tests. The informative experiments are
   `self_join` (transfer of the load boundary and independence of the axes) and
   `hash_probe` (the δ ≈ 0 region and its overlap with Radix).
4. **Is the `self_join` drift an artefact of reservoir sampling?** No. Measured without
   sampling at n = 150/200/223 (fewer than 10⁵ events), δ = +45.3/+45.2/+45.4‰, matching
   the sampled value (+45.1 for n ≥ 300). The drift is also independent of the band
   threshold τ, which changes the number of matches but not the 2n² access trace.
5. **Is `hash_probe` δ ≈ 0 a property of one seed?** No. Across six independent seeds,
   δ ∈ [−0.43, +0.67]‰.

## Limitations

**δ ≈ 0 is not specific to random access.** Radix Sort — a sequential *multi-pass* scan —
measures δ = +3.1‰, inside the ≈ 0 band: each digit pass restarts the index, so the net
trend cancels. A repeated sequential scan is therefore **indistinguishable from random
access** in the `(D, δ)` plane (both at D ≪ 1, δ ≈ 0). The four-family taxonomy thus
yields, in practice, **three regions resolvable by `(D, δ)`**: monotone forward
(δ ≫ 0), root return (δ ≪ 0) and a flat region (δ ≈ 0) containing both random access and
multi-pass scans — intersected by the overload band D > 5. Separating random access from
multi-pass scanning requires an additional descriptor of temporal structure; the
[robustness analysis](../robustness/README.md) (T4) shows that the lag-1 autocorrelation
of the band means does so.

## Threats to validity

- **Temporal precedence of the pre-registration.** The pre-registration, the driver and
  the results were committed together in the original research repository, so the
  version history does not independently establish that the hypotheses preceded the
  measurement.
- **Coverage of the load boundary.** On the D axis, the cross-domain test exercises a
  **single** workload above the boundary (`self_join`); the other three lie far below it.
  H3 is decisive for one family of structural O(n²) workloads, not a sweep of the
  D ≈ 5 boundary outside sorting.
- **Scope of "cross-domain".** The four canonical families are instantiated outside
  sorting, but still on a **single logical array, in memory, single-threaded**. The test
  covers neither workloads with multiple data structures, nor concurrency, nor indices
  reconstructed from virtual addresses (as would be required by eBPF-based capture). It
  is a transfer test of *access pattern*, not of *capture environment*.
